# Reversible private writing Trash

Saved notebook notes, working questions, other private thought types, and saved margin annotations can move to Trash. Legacy record-level reading notes, canonical graph records/claims, source documents, project archives, and recovery copies have their own lifecycles and are not deleted by this feature.

Use **Move to Trash** beside confirmed writing, then **Undo trash** to restore it immediately. Notes and Questions offer a persistent **Trash** view with Restore and original-link actions. Unsaved or pending writing must be saved or explicitly discarded before moving the confirmed item. A trashed direct notebook link shows an unavailable state and Restore; a margin link retains its exact source anchor with a restore action.

Trashing adds a private revisioned visibility record; it does not modify the target's ID, body, source/edition/annotation links, scholarly type, target revision, or original history. Default notebook lists, note-network projections, private note search, and margin lists exclude trashed writing. Incoming references remain intact and may point to an unavailable item until restored. Source quotation/annotation identities remain resolvable for evidence and commentary; hiding private margin writing is not withdrawal of the underlying source.

`GET /v1/writing/trash` pages through private items by project. `PUT` takes the target kind/ID, last confirmed target revision, expected trash revision, and desired state. Edit, trash, and restore serialize on the same target row. Both API checks and PostgreSQL triggers prevent late writes to a currently trashed note; concurrent revisions produce a conflict. A lost acknowledgement can retry the exact request without another state transition. No last-write-wins recovery, public route, automatic publication/withdrawal, permanent deletion, or automatic expiry is introduced.

Trash and its immutable private history are retained until a separately designed purge policy exists. A future archive or Obsidian client must map this visibility history as tombstones without treating it as destructive deletion; full sync remains deferred (#40/#54). Restoring an item whose original source/project is unavailable preserves its links and content; it does not reactivate that source/project or relocate an ambiguous selector.
