# Initial question map

These questions translate the seed workbook into an initial inquiry map. They are prompts, not assumptions about what Islam teaches or what the evidence will show.

## Revelation and knowledge

- How do the Qur'an, hadith traditions, and Muslim theological and philosophical schools describe revelation, prophetic knowledge, reason, and interpretation?
- What kinds of authority do different Muslim communities attribute to scripture, prophetic example, scholars, communal practice, and personal experience?
- How have those accounts changed across periods and settings? Which disagreements concern sources, methods, or underlying theology?

## God, being, and divine action

- How do major Islamic theological, philosophical, and mystical traditions speak about divine unity, attributes, creation, causality, and human language about God?
- What does “God is Being” mean in its original philosophical contexts, and how does it compare with specific Islamic accounts of God and existence?
- Which forms of divine intention or purpose are affirmed, qualified, or rejected by particular authors, and on what grounds?

## Ethics and responsibility

- How do Islamic sources and schools reason about moral knowledge, obligation, justice, suffering, freedom, and responsibility?
- What roles do reason, revelation, law, virtue, intention, consequences, and community play in differing accounts?
- How do these accounts compare with the researcher's tentative constructivism, and where do comparisons fail?

## Diversity, history, and belonging

- How have Muslims interpreted religious difference and pluralism across theological, legal, political, and social contexts?
- Which interpretations are historically situated, and what evidence supports claims about their reception and influence?
- How do lived practice and institutional history complicate abstract accounts of “Islam”?

## Method and self-understanding

- Which of the researcher's starting beliefs are descriptive, normative, metaphysical, or empirically testable?
- What evidence would lead the researcher to revise each belief?
- Where might translation, canon formation, modern categories, selective reading, or outsider/insider standpoint shape the inquiry?
